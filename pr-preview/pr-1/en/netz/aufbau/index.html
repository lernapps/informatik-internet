Is the internet one big cable?

Ben: Is the internet actually a giant cable around the whole world?

Selin: Or a giant computer that holds everything?

Ms Berg: We will rebuild it on project day. Start small: with two devices.

On this page you learn how the internet is built. At the end you find a route through three networks yourself.

Exercise

Mia and Jona sit next to each other. What do their devices need at least so that Mia can send a photo to Jona?

  • A connection, by cable or wireless right answer

    Two devices that are connected can already exchange data. That is the smallest network.

  • The internet

    You need the internet for devices in other networks, far away. For two devices side by side a connection is enough.

  • A printer

    A printer puts data on paper. You do not need one to send a photo.

  • A search engine

    A search engine helps you find web pages. It does not connect devices.

Two connected devices already form a network. By cable or wireless (Wi-Fi) they can exchange data.

router

A device that passes packets on from one cable to the next until they reach their goal. Routers also join networks to each other.

local network

A network in one place, for example at school or in a home. LAN for short (local area network).

Ben is at home, in another network. The routers of the networks are joined, with more routers in between.

Map 3: three networks

✉ The packet says: Photo for Ben's phone

The packet is at Mia's tablet. Press “Next step”.

Map 3: three networks. On the left the school network with Jona's laptop, Mia's tablet and the school router. In the middle a network in between with router 1 and router 2, both joined to the school router and to Ben's router. On the right the network at Ben's home: Ben's router with Ben's phone.

  1. Mia's tablet sends the packet to the school router.
  2. Ben's phone is not in the school network. The school router passes the packet outside, to router 1.
  3. Router 1 passes it on to the router at Ben's home.
  4. Ben's router hands it to Ben's phone. The packet has travelled through three networks.
internet

A network of very many networks all over the world. Routers join the networks and pass packets on from network to network. A network that reaches this far is called a global network.

Remember

The internet is not a cable and not a giant computer, but a network of many networks. Routers pass the packets on. Often there is more than one route: on map 3 the packet could also reach Ben through router 2.

Join in without a computer: the note relay

How to play

  1. Form three groups in three corners of the room. Each group is a network.
  2. Each group picks one child as its router. Only routers walk to other groups.
  3. One child writes a note “For Lea in group 3” and gives it to their router.
  4. The routers pass it on until it reaches Lea.
  5. One router sits down: it is “broken”. Can you find another way?

Exercise

Mia's school router is joined to router 1 and router 2. Both are joined to Ben's router. True or false? If router 1 breaks down, no packet can get from Mia to Ben any more.

Right answer: The statement is false.

The statement is not true. The school router is also joined to router 2, and router 2 to Ben's router. The packet then takes that way. Because there are many ways, the internet does not stop when one router breaks.
